4. WHAT IS A RESEARCH GAP?
==================================================

A research gap is an area where the existing literature indicates:

- insufficient research
- limited evidence
- inconsistent findings
- missing evaluation
- missing datasets
- limited populations
- unexplored methodologies
- insufficient real-world validation
- contradictory findings
- outdated approaches
- unexplored combinations of existing methods

The system must distinguish between:

OBSERVATION

and

VALIDATED RESEARCH GAP.

For example:

Observation:
"Most papers use English datasets."

Potential Gap:
"Multilingual medical RAG is underexplored."

The second statement must be validated against the available papers.

==================================================

==================================================

Never allow the LLM to invent a research gap.

Every research gap must have:

- supporting evidence
- supporting papers
- source chunks
- citations
- evidence explanation

"Insufficient evidence to establish this as a research gap."

Do NOT fabricate:

- papers

- limitations
- citations
- novelty
- research gaps

8. RESEARCH GAP TYPES
==================================================

The system should identify different types of research gaps.

At minimum support:

1. Knowledge Gap
2. Methodological Gap
3. Dataset Gap
4. Evaluation Gap
5. Application Gap
6. Domain Gap
7. Population Gap
8. Temporal Gap
9. Theoretical Gap

11. Implementation Gap
12. Generalization Gap

The LLM may identify additional categories when justified.

Do not force every gap into a predefined category if the evidence suggests another category.

==================================================
9. KNOWLEDGE GAP
==================================================

Identify areas that have not been sufficiently studied.

Example:

Most papers study:

- text-based RAG

Very few study:

- multimodal RAG

Potential gap:

"Multimodal RAG remains insufficiently explored in the reviewed healthcare literature."

This must be evidence-supported.

==================================================
10. METHODOLOGICAL GAP
==================================================

Identify methodological limitations or missing approaches.

Example:

Most studies use:

- dense retrieval

Very few evaluate:

- hybrid retrieval

Potential gap:

"Hybrid retrieval strategies remain insufficiently evaluated in the reviewed setting."

==================================================
11. DATASET GAP
==================================================

Identify missing or limited datasets.

Example:

Most studies use:

- English datasets

Few use:

- multilingual datasets

Potential gap:

"Multilingual evaluation remains limited compared with English-only evaluation."

Do not claim "no multilingual research exists" unless the evidence genuinely supports that.

==================================================
12. EVALUATION GAP
==================================================

Identify missing evaluation dimensions.

Example:

Most papers evaluate:

- accuracy

Few evaluate:

- robustness
- factuality
- latency
- human preference

Potential gap:

"Current evaluations place greater emphasis on accuracy than robustness and human-centered evaluation."

==================================================
13. APPLICATION GAP
==================================================

Identify missing real-world validation.

Example:

Most research:

- benchmark datasets
- simulation

Few studies:

- real-world deployment

Potential gap:

"Real-world validation remains limited."

==================================================
14. DOMAIN GAP
==================================================

Identify domains that are insufficiently represented.

Example:

Research focuses on:

- general healthcare

Limited:

- emergency medicine

Potential gap:

"Emergency-care applications remain comparatively underexplored."

==================================================
15. POPULATION GAP
==================================================

Identify underrepresented populations.

Example:

Research focuses on:

- adults

Limited:

- pediatric populations

The system must only make such claims if the papers contain appropriate population information.

==================================================
16. TEMPORAL GAP
==================================================

Identify areas where research may not have kept pace with newer approaches.

Example:

Most papers evaluate older architectures while very recent approaches are not sufficiently evaluated.

Use actual publication dates and paper metadata.

Do not make unsupported claims such as:

"Nobody has studied this since 2023."

==================================================
17. THEORETICAL GAP
==================================================

Identify missing conceptual or theoretical understanding.

Example:

Multiple papers report different results, but there is insufficient explanation of why.

Potential gap:

"The factors responsible for differing retrieval effectiveness remain insufficiently understood."

This type of gap must be carefully grounded.

==================================================
18. CONTRADICTION GAP
==================================================

This is an important feature.

Suppose:

Paper A:
Method improves performance.

Paper B:
Method improves performance.

Paper C:
Method produces no improvement.

The system should identify:

"Existing studies report inconsistent findings regarding the effectiveness of the method."

Then investigate:

- datasets
- models
- retrieval methods
- evaluation metrics
- experimental conditions

Do not automatically assume why the results differ.

==================================================
19. GENERALIZATION GAP
==================================================

Identify when research works only under narrow conditions.

Example:

Research has been evaluated on:

- one dataset
- one language
- one domain

Potential gap:

"Generalization across datasets and domains remains insufficiently evaluated."

==================================================
20. IMPLEMENTATION GAP
==================================================

Identify gaps between research prototypes and practical systems.

Example:

Research papers report model performance but provide limited information about:

- latency
- scalability
- cost
- deployment
- reliability

Potential gap:

"Production-level evaluation remains limited."

29. COUNTER-EVIDENCE SEARCH
==================================================

For every candidate gap, actively search for evidence that contradicts it.

Example:

Candidate:

"Multilingual evaluation is missing."

Counter search finds:

Paper X:
"We evaluate on English, Spanish, and French datasets."

Therefore:

The original candidate must be weakened or rejected.

Possible final statement:

"Multilingual evaluation exists but remains limited relative to English-only evaluation."

This is mandatory.

==================================================
30. GAP VALIDATION
==================================================

Each candidate must pass validation.

Validation should consider:

1. Evidence exists?
2. Multiple papers support it?
3. Counter-evidence exists?
4. How significant is the counter-evidence?
5. Is the claim too strong?
6. Can the statement be safely supported?
7. Is the gap already adequately studied?

Possible results:

VALIDATED
PARTIALLY VALIDATED
REJECTED
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

==================================================
31. GAP REFINEMENT
==================================================

If the original candidate is too strong:

Original:

"No research exists on multilingual RAG."

Evidence:

3 multilingual papers exist.

Refine:

"Multilingual RAG remains less extensively evaluated than English-only RAG in the reviewed literature."

The system should prefer accurate weaker claims over unsupported strong claims.
