# 1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Transform the AI Research Assistant from a system that can:

* answer questions about papers
* summarize papers
* compare multiple papers

into a system that can automatically generate a **research-grounded literature review across a collection of papers**.

The user should be able to provide a research question such as:

"Review recent research on Retrieval-Augmented Generation for medical question answering."

The system should:

1. Understand the research question.
2. Identify relevant papers from the workspace.
3. Rank papers by relevance.
4. Retrieve evidence from relevant papers.
5. Extract important findings from each paper.
6. Discover major research themes.
7. Group evidence by theme.
8. Synthesize findings across papers.
9. Identify agreements.
10. Identify disagreements and contradictions.
11. Identify methodological trends.
12. Identify common limitations.
13. Identify emerging directions.
14. Generate an academic-style literature review.
15. Attach citations to factual claims.
16. Verify citations.
17. Perform a quality and grounding check.
18. Save the literature review.
19. Allow the user to view previous literature reviews.

The system must remain grounded in the uploaded research papers.

# 5. WHAT IS AUTOMATED LITERATURE REVIEW?

The system should answer a higher-level question:

"What does the existing research say about this topic?"

It should not simply summarize each paper individually.

It must perform:

```text
Research Question
        ↓
Relevant Papers
        ↓
Evidence Extraction
        ↓
Theme Discovery
        ↓
Cross-Paper Synthesis
        ↓
Agreement / Disagreement Analysis
        ↓
Trend Analysis
        ↓
Limitations
        ↓
Emerging Directions
        ↓
Literature Review
        ↓
Citation Verification
```

The final output should represent the **research landscape**, not a list of independent paper summaries.

* different datasets
* different models
* different evaluation metrics
* different retrieval strategies
* different experimental settings

Do not assume a reason unless supported by evidence.

# 26. HIERARCHICAL SYNTHESIS

This is a critical architectural requirement.

Do NOT send 20–50 entire papers into one LLM context.

Use hierarchical processing:

```text
Papers
   ↓
Relevant Evidence
   ↓
Paper-Level Insights
   ↓
Theme-Level Groups
   ↓
Theme-Level Synthesis
   ↓
Cross-Theme Synthesis
   ↓
Final Literature Review
```

This reduces:

* token usage
* latency
* hallucination
* context overflow
* cost

and improves reasoning quality.


# 34. ANTI-HALLUCINATION

The system must never invent:

* papers
* authors
* publication years
* datasets
* results
* metrics
* architectures
* limitations
* research trends
* citations
* consensus

"The available papers do not provide sufficient evidence to establish this conclusion."

---

# 35. BALANCED SYNTHESIS

The literature review should not automatically favor the majority finding.

For example:

If:

```text
8 papers support method A
2 papers report no improvement
```

the review should acknowledge both.

Use language such as:

"Most of the reviewed studies report improvements, although two studies report limited or insignificant gains under their evaluation settings."

Cite both sides.

---

# 36. AVOID PAPER-BY-PAPER REPETITION

Do not generate:

```text
Paper A says...
Paper B says...
Paper C says...
Paper D says...
```

for the entire review.

Instead synthesize:

```text
Several studies use...
Multiple papers report...
However, some studies find...
```

Then provide citations.

Paper-level summaries may still be available as supporting information.
